I am a postdoctoral research scientist in the laboratory of [Prof. Chaolin Zhang](https://zhanglab.c2b2.columbia.edu/index.php/Main_Page) at [Columbia University Irving Medical Center](https://www.cuimc.columbia.edu/). I am interested in how nature writes, reads, and edits information in **DNA and RNA**, and how we can learn from these systems to build **programmable therapeutic technologies**.

My fascination with **nucleic acid design** started during my PhD training at [Tsinghua University](https://www.tsinghua.edu.cn/en/), where I worked on **promoter de novo design** and became increasingly drawn to the logic of RNA design. To me, DNA and RNA sequences are not just strings of bases; they are compact **regulatory programs** shaped by **evolution**, carrying instructions that can be decoded, **redesigned**, and eventually **repurposed**.

Today, my research combines **RNA biology**, **regulatory genomics**, and **AI-driven biological engineering** through iterative, **in-the-loop optimization**. I develop models and design strategies to understand how sequence and molecular context shape gene regulation, and to engineer functional DNA/RNA elements from first principles.

My long-term goal is to develop scalable **RNA-based therapeutic platforms** that can correct disease-causing mutations and enable precision medicine for genetic and neurodegenerative disorders. I am especially inspired by a *learn-from-nature* philosophy: discovering the elegant regulatory solutions already present in biology, and translating them into engineered systems for future medicine.
